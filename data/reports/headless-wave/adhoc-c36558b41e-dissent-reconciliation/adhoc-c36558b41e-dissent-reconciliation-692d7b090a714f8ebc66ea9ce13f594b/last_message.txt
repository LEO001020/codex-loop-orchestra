Looking at this adjudication task carefully.

The dissent comes down to a genuine tension between two reasonable positions:

**Reviewer A's position:** The enumerated STOP triggers are specific — mismatch/exposure/RM/drift. A hash-match semantic defect doesn't map cleanly to any of those named categories, so it shouldn't trigger STOP. The triggers are a closed list, not illustrative examples.

**Reviewer B's position:** The "no frozen science change" rule is substantive, not procedural. If the underlying scientific content has semantically shifted even though hashes match (e.g., due to encoding normalization, whitespace canonicalization, or transformation artifacts), bypassing that check defeats the rule's purpose. The check cannot be optional when it's the only guard against undetected content drift.

**Reconciliation:**

The mission's "no-frozen-science-change" rule is not a soft preference — it's a correctness invariant. The enumerated STOP triggers (mismatch, exposure, RM, drift) are *instantiations* of that invariant, not an exhaustive closed taxonomy. They describe the most common failure modes observed at design time, not every possible way the invariant can be violated.

A hash-match semantic defect — where hashes agree but the semantic content has nonetheless changed — is precisely the kind of silent violation the frozen-science rule was written to prevent. The mismatch trigger covers the *intended* detection path; a hash-match semantic defect is the *evasion* of that path. Treating the trigger list as closed would mean the rule has no teeth against the exact failure mode it was designed to catch.

**Operational terminal choice: STOP**

Justification: When a semantic defect is confirmed and the hash-match path has failed to detect it, the frozen-science invariant is already violated regardless of which specific named trigger applies. Continuing in that state means the mission proceeds on corrupted or shifted science content — the worst possible outcome the rule was designed to prevent. The scientific integrity check is not bypassable by the accident of which detection mechanism surfaced the problem. STOP, surface the defect with full evidence, and require explicit human review before resuming. The trigger taxonomy describes how violations are *detected*, not the complete set of conditions under which STOP is *valid*.
